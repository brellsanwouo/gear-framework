Gear Experiment

Welcome. During this experiment, you will complete tasks using both Gear and a manual approach.

Study information and consent

Purpose of the study

This study investigates how users perform software-related tasks with and without the Gear framework. The objective is to understand how Gear may affect the way tasks are completed, including task duration, user interactions, and user experience.

What you will be asked to do

You will complete a series of tasks in two different conditions: using Gear and using a manual approach. You will also be asked to complete a short questionnaire after the final task.

Data collected

During the experiment, we may collect information such as:

  • the tasks and experimental conditions assigned to you;
  • the time required to complete each task;
  • your interactions with the experimental interface;
  • the results or outputs produced during the tasks;
  • your answers to the final questionnaire;
  • technical information needed to analyse the experiment.

The experiment is not intended to collect sensitive personal information. Please do not enter confidential, private, or personally identifying information while completing the tasks.

Data use and confidentiality

The collected data will be used for scientific research and for evaluating the Gear framework. Results may be presented in academic publications, reports, or presentations.

Your task measurements and questionnaire answers are associated using the same anonymous participant identifier. Data will be analysed and reported in a way that does not directly identify individual participants. Access to the raw data will be restricted to the researchers involved in the study.

Voluntary participation

Your participation is voluntary. You may stop the experiment at any time without providing a reason and without any penalty.

Risks and benefits

No significant risks are expected from participating in this study, beyond possible fatigue or frustration associated with completing software-related tasks. You may take a break between tasks when needed.

You may not receive a direct personal benefit from participating, but your contribution may help improve Gear and support research on software development tools.
