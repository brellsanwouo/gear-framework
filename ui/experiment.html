Gear Experiment

Welcome. You will be assigned to one method, either Gear Studio or manual Python. After training and familiarization, you will implement the same two measured tasks in two agent frameworks.

Study information and consent

Purpose of the study

This study investigates how a visual configuration approach and a manual Python approach support the implementation of multi-agent systems across different agent frameworks.

What you will be asked to do

You will be assigned to either Gear Studio or manual Python for the entire experiment. You will first complete an unmeasured training task and a familiarization task in the first framework. You will then complete two measured tasks involving parallelism and loops, before adapting those same two tasks to a second framework. You will also complete a questionnaire after the final task.

Data collected

During the experiment, we may collect:

  • the method, framework order, task order, and study phase assigned to you;
  • the time required to complete each task;
  • whether a task was confirmed voluntarily or ended because time expired;
  • the configurations, code, executions, and outputs produced during the tasks;
  • your answers to the final questionnaire;
  • technical information needed to analyse the experiment.

The experiment is not intended to collect sensitive personal information. Please do not enter confidential, private, or personally identifying information while completing the tasks.

Data use and confidentiality

The collected data will be used for scientific research and for evaluating Gear. Results may be presented in academic publications, reports, or presentations.

Your task measurements and questionnaire answers are associated using the same anonymous participant identifier. Data will be analysed and reported without directly identifying individual participants. Access to raw data is restricted to the researchers involved in the study.

Voluntary participation

Your participation is voluntary. You may stop the experiment at any time without providing a reason and without any penalty.

Risks and benefits

No significant risks are expected beyond possible fatigue or frustration associated with software-related tasks. You may take a break between tasks.

You may not receive a direct personal benefit, but your contribution may help improve Gear and support research on multi-agent development tools.
