Gear Experiment

Welcome. You will be assigned to one method, either Gear Studio or manual Python. After training and familiarization, you will implement the same two measured tasks in two agent frameworks.

Study information and consent

Purpose of the study

This study investigates how a visual configuration approach and a manual Python approach support the implementation of multi-agent systems across different agent frameworks.

What you will be asked to do

You will be assigned to either Gear Studio or manual Python for the entire experiment. You will first complete an unmeasured training task and a familiarization task in the first framework. You will then complete two measured tasks before adapting those same tasks to a second framework. Short questionnaires are presented after tasks and frameworks, followed by a final questionnaire and written debriefing.

Data collected

During the experiment, we may collect:

  • the method, framework order, task order, and study phase assigned to you;
  • the time required to complete each task and any recorded technical pauses;
  • whether a task was confirmed, timed out, or ended because of a technical failure;
  • the configurations, code, executions, outputs, model usage, tokens, and estimated costs;
  • your questionnaire and written debriefing responses;
  • an operator report documenting protocol deviations or technical incidents.

The experiment is not intended to collect sensitive personal information. Please do not enter confidential, private, or personally identifying information while completing the tasks or questionnaires.

Data use and confidentiality

The collected data will be used for scientific research and for evaluating Gear. Results may be presented in academic publications, reports, or presentations.

Task measurements, questionnaire answers, and the operator report are linked using the same pseudonymous study code. Data will be analysed and reported without directly identifying individual participants. Access to raw data is restricted to the researchers involved in the study.

Voluntary participation

Your participation is voluntary. You may stop the experiment at any time without providing a reason and without any penalty.

Risks and benefits

No significant risks are expected beyond possible fatigue or frustration associated with software-related tasks. Technical interruptions may be paused by the experimenter so that they do not reduce your active task time.
